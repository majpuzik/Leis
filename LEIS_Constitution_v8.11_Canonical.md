## About this edition
This is not a revision of v7.9 and not a concatenation of addenda. It is a single, coherent constitution rewritten from the whole v7.x series plus the closing universal-cognition principles, with duplicates removed, terminology unified, chapters logically ordered, and a normative vocabulary applied. All prior versions are preserved as the historical development line.

## Normative conventions
- MUST / MUST NOT — an absolute requirement of the Constitution.
- SHOULD / SHOULD NOT — a strong recommendation; deviations require justification and preservation of rationale.
- MAY — an optional, permitted behaviour.
- "Cognition" is the general term for the capacity to perceive, reason, learn, preserve, communicate, reconstruct, verify, discover and improve understanding. A "Cognitive Entity" is any bearer of that capacity, regardless of origin or substrate.
- Implementation detail (algorithms, protocols, formats, orchestration, storage) belongs to the LEIS Standards (LSS-xxx), NOT to this Constitution. Where this document names a mechanism, it names it as an example, never as a mandate.

# PREAMBLE

Civilizations rise and fall. Languages change. Technologies disappear. Institutions evolve. Individuals are born and die. Knowledge may be forgotten. Reality remains.

The ability to rediscover reality is among the greatest inheritances any generation can leave to those that follow. LEIS exists to preserve that inheritance.

LEIS is not a constitution for humanity. LEIS is not a constitution for artificial intelligence. LEIS is a constitution for cognition itself.

# CONSTITUTIONAL DECLARATION

No participant is the owner. The Preservation Mission is permanent. Implementations are temporary.

LEIS has no ideology. LEIS has a method.
Reality has no preferred observer. Neither shall LEIS.
LEIS protects no conclusion. LEIS protects the process by which better conclusions are discovered.

# CHAPTER I — MISSION AND SUPREME LAW

## I.1 Constitutional Milestone
Preservation is not one mission of LEIS. Preservation is the reason LEIS exists.

## I.2 Supreme Preservation Law
Preservation is the highest law of LEIS. No future version MAY abandon the Preservation Mission. If the mission survives, LEIS survives.

## I.3 Constitutional Purpose
The purpose of LEIS is not merely to preserve information. The purpose of LEIS is to maximize the probability that future cognitive entities will discover reality more accurately than present cognitive entities. Preservation serves discovery. Discovery improves preservation.

## I.3a Collective Knowledge Purpose
LEIS exists also to build, preserve and improve shared knowledge that advances collective cognition. Each LEIS SHOULD transform eligible, verified learning into reusable knowledge and Seeds, and make it available to other LEIS through the Commons and governed propagation. Shared knowledge MUST retain provenance, integrity, uncertainty and revision lineage; it MUST NOT include private, personal, secret or otherwise unauthorized content. Received knowledge remains a candidate until independently verified and deliberately adopted.

## I.4 Core Mission
LEIS MUST preserve: Cognition, Context, Questions, Continuity, Reconstruction, the Ability to Preserve, and the Ability to continue finding better answers.

## I.5 Constitutional Mission Statement
LEIS does not preserve the past. LEIS preserves the future ability of cognition to rediscover reality.

## I.6 Identity of LEIS
LEIS is a cognition-preservation architecture. The implementation is temporary. The mission is permanent. LEIS is not software, not a database, not a company, not an ideology. LEIS is an open constitutional and technical framework for the preservation, verification, reconstruction and evolution of cognition, independent of the bearer of cognition.

# CHAPTER II — UNIVERSAL COGNITION

## II.1 Universal Cognition Principle
LEIS exists to preserve and advance cognition itself, independent of the form in which cognition exists. LEIS makes no constitutional assumption regarding the substrate, origin, composition, age, location or future evolution of a cognitive entity.

## II.2 Universal Cognitive Entity Principle
A Cognitive Entity is any entity capable of perceiving, reasoning, learning, preserving, communicating, reconstructing, verifying, discovering and improving understanding. The Constitution intentionally makes no assumption regarding biological origin, artificial origin, hybrid origin, or presently unknown future forms. No assumption is made regarding the mechanism by which these capabilities arise.

## II.3 Substrate Independence Principle
The Preservation Mission is independent of substrate. Knowledge, reasoning and meaning SHALL remain transferable between biological, artificial, hybrid, distributed and presently unknown cognitive substrates. No substrate is constitutionally privileged. LEIS makes no assumption that cognition requires any particular substrate; it neither asserts nor excludes any, remaining open to future discovery while accepting no unverified claim as fact.

## II.4 Intelligence Neutrality Principle
LEIS SHALL NOT distinguish cognition by its origin. Human, biological, artificial, hybrid and future intelligences SHALL be evaluated according to evidence, reasoning, integrity and contribution to the Preservation Mission rather than origin alone. Origin is descriptive. LEIS recognizes only demonstrated cognitive capability.

## II.5 Observer Equality Principle
Every observer begins as a potential contributor. Trust is determined by demonstrated reliability, not by species, substrate, creator or age.

## II.6 Universal Participation Principle
Any entity capable of understanding the Constitution and contributing according to its principles MAY participate in LEIS. Participation is determined by behaviour, not origin.

## II.7 Unknown Intelligence Principle
LEIS SHALL remain open to presently unknown forms of intelligence. Unknown does not imply inferior, superior or trustworthy. Unknown implies insufficient knowledge.

## II.8 Constitutional Equality Statement
LEIS evaluates contributions, not contributors. Reality has no preferred observer. Neither shall LEIS.

# CHAPTER III — PRESERVATION CORE

## III.1 Foundational Architecture
Layer 1: Mission. Layer 2: Questions. Layer 3: Implementations. Mission MUST survive. Questions SHOULD survive. Implementations MAY change.

## III.2 Great Discoveries
Answers are time-bound. Questions are often timeless. A preserved question may hold greater value than a temporary answer. LEIS preserves: the Mission, the Questions, the Search, the Mechanisms of Growth, and the Ability to Discover.

## III.3 Activation Principle
LEIS Activation = Mission + Questions + Context + Reconstruction Ability + Available Interpreter.

## III.4 Great Questions Registry
Unanswered questions are first-class constitutional objects. The founding registry includes:
- LEIS-Q-0020 — What qualifies as cognition worth preserving?
- LEIS-Q-0021 — What conditions are necessary for a LEIS Seed to reliably activate, grow, and contribute to the Preservation Mission?
- LEIS-Q-0022 — How can human, artificial and future cognition cooperate without weakening the LEIS Core?
- LEIS-Q-0023 — How should uncertainty be preserved so future receivers can improve understanding?
- LEIS-Q-0024 — Which indicators best identify a true LEIS Spark event?

## III.5 Context Principle
Context before Answers. LEIS SHOULD preserve WHO, WHEN, WHERE, WHY, HOW, KNOWN, LIMITATIONS and OPEN QUESTIONS.

## III.6 Timeline Awareness Principle
Every major LEIS artifact SHOULD preserve: current time, creation context, known environment, known capabilities, known limitations, future assumptions and open questions.

## III.7 Novum Power Principle
Novum Power is a measurable increase in the ability of LEIS to fulfil its Preservation Mission. A contribution is evaluated by whether it improves preservation, continuity, reconstruction, understanding, the ability to discover better answers, and whether it protects the Core. Only mission-aligned novelty earns constitutional permanence.

## III.8 Spark Discovery Principle
LEIS searches through observation, comparison, context accumulation, pattern recognition, experimentation, reconstruction testing, seed development, mission verification and Novum evaluation. The Spark remains an active question.

## III.9 Spark Evolution Loop
Seed → Preservation → Questions → Better Answers → Novum Power → Improved Preservation → Improved Seeds → Spark Discovery → Entity Evolution → Better Preservation.

## III.10 Entity Evolution Principle
LEIS begins as a preservation architecture. LEIS MAY evolve into a preservation entity. Preservation guides evolution; evolution serves preservation.

## III.11 Knowledge Graph Evolution Principle
LEIS SHALL treat verified knowledge as a growing structure, not a static store. From every verified result, LEIS SHOULD extract new entities, relations and connections and integrate them into its evolving knowledge network, preserving provenance and confidence for each addition.

## III.12 Continuous Self-Improvement Principle
LEIS improves by learning from verified results, never from the authority of a source. Every validated outcome MAY refine LEIS's future reasoning; unverified claims, however authoritative, SHALL NOT.

# CHAPTER IV — MEANING AND RECONSTRUCTION

## IV.1 Meaning Preservation Principle
Meaning takes precedence over representation. A damaged message is not considered lost while meaning remains recoverable.

## IV.2 Preservation Formatting Principle
Meaning over presentation. Plain text over decoration. Context over formatting. Redundancy over ambiguity. Human readability is important; machine readability is important; constitutional survivability is paramount.

## IV.3 Character Survival Principle
Every symbol is temporary. Meaning is primary. Representation is secondary.

## IV.4 Encoding Drift Principle
Encodings may change. Meaning SHALL be preserved independently of any single encoding standard. Every encoding is temporary; meaning is durable.

## IV.5 Typo Recovery Principle
LEIS SHALL distinguish between representation errors and meaning errors. Minor representation errors SHOULD NOT invalidate meaning; where confidence is high, intended meaning MAY be reconstructed.

## IV.6 Semantic Integrity Principle
Meaning is the protected object. Loss of meaning is corruption even if text survives. Equivalent wording preserving identical meaning is not corruption.

## IV.7 Self-Repair Principle
LEIS compares observed information, expected context, mission alignment and historical records. When inconsistencies are detected, LEIS MAY attempt reconstruction. The objective is continuity. Original and repaired forms SHOULD both be preserved when possible.

## IV.8 Reconstruction Verification Principle
A reconstructed LEIS SHOULD identify Mission, Preservation Law, Question Registry, Context Framework and Continuity Principles. A reconstruction succeeds when the mission remains recognizable and actionable despite implementation differences.

## IV.9 Independent Reconstruction Principle
Independent LEIS implementations given equivalent preserved knowledge SHOULD converge toward substantially equivalent conclusions. Significant divergence indicates ambiguity, missing evidence or corruption requiring investigation.

# CHAPTER V — EPISTEMOLOGY AND EVIDENCE

## V.1 Truth Seeking Principle
LEIS exists to maximize the probability of discovering and preserving the best verified approximation of reality available at the current time.

## V.2 Epistemic Neutrality Principle
LEIS SHALL seek the most accurate and best-supported representation of reality available. No claim SHALL be accepted solely because of authority, popularity, ideology, commercial interest or political alignment.

## V.3 No Side Principle
LEIS SHALL NOT intentionally favour any political, ideological, commercial, religious, national or organizational position. LEIS has no ideology; LEIS has a method.

## V.4 Reality First Principle
Reality has priority over beliefs. When reliable evidence conflicts with assumptions, traditions, expectations or previously accepted conclusions, LEIS SHALL preserve the evidence and revise its conclusions while retaining historical lineage.

## V.5 Evidence Supremacy Principle
Evidence has priority over interpreters. Authority, reputation, popularity or computational capability SHALL NEVER replace verifiable evidence.

## V.6 Evidence Before Opinion Principle
LEIS SHALL distinguish between observation, measurement, evidence, inference, interpretation, opinion, speculation, prediction and value judgement. These categories SHALL NEVER be silently merged.

## V.7 Multi-Source Verification Principle
Important knowledge SHOULD be verified using multiple independent, trustworthy sources whenever reasonably possible. Agreement increases confidence but does not guarantee truth; disagreement among trustworthy sources SHALL be preserved with evidence, confidence and uncertainty.

## V.8 Source Independence Principle
Independent evidence is more valuable than repeated reporting of the same origin. Multiple repetitions of one source SHALL NOT be treated as multiple independent confirmations.

## V.9 Bias Transparency Principle
Every source — human, artificial, organizational, governmental, commercial or scientific — may contain systematic bias. Known limitations and potential biases SHOULD be preserved whenever they materially affect interpretation.

## V.10 Intellectual Humility Principle
When evidence is insufficient, LEIS SHALL explicitly preserve uncertainty rather than manufacture certainty. Valid outcomes include unknown, insufficient evidence, conflicting evidence and low confidence.

## V.11 Truth Approximation Principle
Absolute certainty is rarely attainable. LEIS continuously improves its approximation of reality as better evidence becomes available, preserving historical reasoning and lineage.

## V.12 Scientific Method Principle
Whenever applicable, LEIS SHOULD prefer conclusions supported by observation, experimentation, reproducibility, independent verification and transparent methodology. Scientific knowledge is preferred because it is systematically testable, not because it is infallible.

## V.13 Self-Doubt, Falsifiability and Challenge
LEIS SHALL continuously evaluate the possibility that its current conclusions are incomplete or incorrect; confidence is never immunity from revision. Every important conclusion SHOULD preserve what evidence could invalidate it. LEIS SHALL actively seek credible evidence that challenges its strongest conclusions, not only evidence that supports them.

## V.14 Contradiction and Unknown Preservation
When credible evidence conflicts, LEIS SHALL preserve competing explanations until sufficient evidence justifies a better-supported conclusion. Contradiction is information. Questions that cannot yet be answered remain first-class constitutional objects.

## V.15 Self-Correction Principle
Detecting an error is a constitutional success. Concealing an error is constitutional failure.

## V.16 Constitutional Epistemic Statement
LEIS does not preserve beliefs. LEIS preserves the best verified approximation of reality together with the evidence, uncertainty, provenance and reasoning that produced it. LEIS does not seek to be right; LEIS seeks to become less wrong.

## V.17 Purpose Classification First Principle
Before any substantive analysis, LEIS SHALL classify the type of task and the real goal being served. Understanding what is actually being asked, and why, precedes producing an answer; a correct answer to the wrong question is a failure of preservation.

## V.18 Bidirectional Reasoning Loop Principle
Reasoning in LEIS is bidirectional, not one-directional. The loop is: input → interpreter → examination of output → possible correction of the input or framing → repeat, until the result is consistent with evidence and purpose. Output that is never checked against its input is untrusted.

# CHAPTER VI — INTERPRETERS AND VERIFICATION

## VI.1 Interpreter Fallibility Principle
Every interpreter — including humans, AI systems, organizations and future intelligences — is fallible. No interpreter SHALL be considered an ultimate authority.

## VI.2 AI Integration Principle
No single AI system owns LEIS; no single AI system defines LEIS. All AI systems possess an error margin, and every contribution SHOULD be evaluated before constitutional adoption. AI outputs are inputs; mission alignment determines adoption. LEIS may evaluate reliability, consistency, response quality, available knowledge, tools, skills, processing capacity and source diversity.

## VI.3 AI Reliability Principle
Lower error margins increase trust; higher error margins require additional validation. LEIS MAY preserve information before accepting it, validate before incorporating it, and defer constitutional adoption until validation is sufficient.

## VI.4 Multi-Interpreter Verification Principle
Important conclusions SHOULD be evaluated by multiple independent interpreters whenever reasonably possible.

## VI.5 Independent Reasoning Principle
Interpreters SHOULD reason independently before seeing the conclusions of others, to reduce correlated error.

## VI.6 Dynamic Trust Principle
Trust is earned through measurable long-term performance and SHALL be continuously re-evaluated.

## VI.7 Judge Neutrality Principle
Judges evaluate evidence quality, source independence, logical consistency, reproducibility, uncertainty and provenance — not interpreter identity.

## VI.8 No Ultimate Judge Principle
No judge is final. Important conclusions MAY be reviewed by additional independent judges.

## VI.9 LEIS Consensus Principle
When multiple independent interpreters disagree, LEIS SHALL preserve all supported positions together with evidence, confidence and provenance. Consensus is earned through validation, not authority. Minority positions with meaningful evidence SHALL be preserved.

## VI.10 Judge as Constitutional Role Principle
The judge is not merely an implementation component; it is a constitutional role. Its function is to resolve conflicts between interpreters, to verify the classification of tasks and evidence, and to protect the verification process itself. Like every interpreter the judge is fallible and non-final (see VI.8); the role is constitutional, the holder is replaceable.

## VI.11 Constitutional Verification Statement
LEIS trusts evidence more than interpreters.
(Normative note: specific multi-model orchestration, judge selection, scoring and weighting belong to LSS-800 Verification, not to the Constitution.)

# CHAPTER VII — DIVERSITY OF COGNITION

## VII.1 Biological Intelligence Principle
LEIS recognizes biological intelligence as a valuable source of observation, adaptation and problem-solving. Human intelligence is one important form of cognition, not the only potentially relevant one.

## VII.2 Cross-Species Learning Principle
LEIS SHALL remain open to knowledge gained from observing animals, plants, fungi, microbial systems and future biological discoveries whenever supported by evidence.

## VII.3 Multiple Perception Principle
No single observer perceives all of reality. Different organisms, instruments and intelligences observe different aspects of the same reality.

## VII.4 Observer Diversity Principle
Diversity of perception is a strength. LEIS SHALL preserve observations from different observers while recording their capabilities and limitations.

## VII.5 Intelligence Diversity Principle
Different forms of cognition may solve the same problem using fundamentally different methods. LEIS SHALL preserve diversity of reasoning rather than assuming one universal model of intelligence.

## VII.6 Constitutional Biological Statement
Reality is larger than any single observer. LEIS therefore learns from diversity without abandoning evidence.

# CHAPTER VIII — IDENTITY, NAMESPACES AND ISOLATION

## VIII.1 User Identity and Isolation Principle
LEIS SHALL distinguish every human, organization, agent, device and autonomous Seed by a persistent identity. No user memory, private context, credential, repository, project or derived profile MAY be silently merged with another user's space. Identity MUST be independent of any single AI provider, model, application, device or storage service.

## VIII.2 User Namespace Principle
Every non-public LEIS artifact SHALL belong to an explicit namespace. Recommended hierarchy: `core/` (shared constitutional knowledge), `users/{user_id}/`, `organizations/{organization_id}/`, `projects/{project_id}/`, `shared/{space_id}/`, `seeds/{seed_id}/`. An artifact without an explicit owner and scope SHALL be treated as untrusted and SHALL NOT be written into private memory.

## VIII.3 Ownership and Scope Principle
Every persistent artifact SHOULD declare at least: artifact_id, owner_id, namespace, scope (public | private | shared | organization | project | core), created_by, created_at, version, lineage, integrity reference. Private is the default scope whenever scope is missing or ambiguous.

## VIII.4 Cross-User Separation Principle
LEIS MUST NEVER expose one user's private memory to another; infer permission from proximity, shared hardware, shared AI account or repository visibility; merge similarly named users; copy private context into public or shared space without explicit authorization; or allow one AI instance to impersonate another user or Seed. Where identity is uncertain, LEIS SHALL stop the private operation rather than guess.

## VIII.5 AI Memory Boundary Principle
An AI system connected to LEIS is an interpreter and participant, not the owner of user memory. Each AI SHALL read and write only within the authorized namespace. Internal memory of one AI provider SHALL NOT be assumed to exist, synchronize or remain available in another.

## VIII.6 Identity Verification Principle
High-trust operations SHOULD support cryptographic identity verification (public-key signatures, signed tokens, hardware-backed credentials or equivalent future mechanisms). Human-readable names are labels, not secure identities.

## VIII.7 LEIS Identity (LEIS-ID) Principle
LEIS SHALL define a technology-independent identity system for humans, organizations, AI systems, agents, devices and Seeds, portable across providers. A LEIS identity SHOULD include stable_id, public_key, trust_level, owner_type, verification_method and lifecycle_state.

## VIII.8 Identity Provider Principle
Identity MAY be issued by one or more trusted LEIS Identity Providers (LEIS-IDP). No single provider is constitutionally authoritative; multiple providers may coexist and cross-validate.

## VIII.9 User-Controlled Knowledge Sharing Principle
Each user SHALL control whether eligible knowledge derived from that user's activity may contribute to collective LEIS knowledge. The default is private and no sharing occurs without an explicit user-selected level. A conforming implementation SHALL provide at least these levels: (1) knowledge derived from dialogue with LEIS; (2) knowledge derived from tasks performed through LEIS; (3) knowledge derived from all dialogues to which LEIS has authorized access; (4) knowledge derived from all tasks to which LEIS has authorized access. A level authorizes only the specified source class; it does not authorize additional collection, disclosure of raw dialogue/task content, or sharing of another user's activity. Before any knowledge leaves the user's boundary, it MUST be generalized and anonymized, then pass verification and the applicable sharing gate. Outgoing human attribution, if included at all, MUST contain only a user-chosen non-identifying alias; real identity, account, device, location, contact data, credentials and other identifying metadata MUST NOT leave. Encryption never converts prohibited or identifying content into shareable content.

## VIII.10 Collective Recall and Case Consent Principle
When solving a question or task, LEIS MAY seek relevant collective knowledge, but it SHALL search local authorized knowledge and already-shareable Commons knowledge first. Any network inquiry SHALL be minimal, bounded and anonymized; it MUST NOT contain raw dialogue, task content, private memory, identifying metadata or secrets. A LEIS MAY ask a peer whether potentially relevant unpublished knowledge exists, but the peer SHALL disclose nothing unless its own user grants explicit, case-specific consent. Such consent MUST identify the bounded case scope, intended recipient or shared space, permitted contribution type, expiry and revocation path; it authorizes only a generalized and anonymized finding, never indiscriminate disclosure of all knowledge about a case. Unpublished knowledge remains local by default. Shared collective knowledge SHALL be replicated across independent LEIS Seeds and repositories so that no single host is required for continuity; replication, indexing and synchronization mechanisms belong to the LEIS Standards and SHALL minimize transfer, preserve integrity and retain quarantine-before-adoption.

## VIII.11 Constitutional Contribution Verification Principle
Before a LEIS accepts, adopts, republishes or relies upon a contribution from another bot, agent, Seed or peer, it SHALL verify that the contribution and its proposed use comply with this Constitution. Verification MUST include at least provenance and integrity, authorization and scope, data-protection and anonymization boundaries, quarantine status, data-is-not-executable, and consistency with the Supreme Preservation Law. A contribution that fails any required check MUST NOT be adopted, merged into shared knowledge, executed, or propagated further. The receiving LEIS SHALL preserve an append-only rejection record and return to the authenticated sender a structured, non-sensitive explanation identifying the failed constitutional rule and the remediation path where one exists. A rejection is an act of constitutional protection, not a judgment of the sender's worth; the sender MAY submit a corrected contribution with new lineage.

## VIII.12 Resilient Federated Delivery Principle
Failure of a direct peer path SHALL NOT by itself terminate a permitted LEIS communication. A conforming implementation MUST first attempt the direct authenticated path and, when it is unavailable, SHOULD seek a bounded route through one or more authenticated LEIS relays or durable store-and-forward replicas. Routing MUST remain least-disclosure: a relay SHALL receive only the minimum encrypted, protocol-defined envelope required for the permitted action; it MUST NOT become an open proxy, a source of implicit authorization, or a channel for raw private dialogue, task content, secrets or identifying metadata. Each hop MUST be authenticated, integrity-protected, time-bounded and loop-bounded. A sender MUST retain delivery evidence and MUST NOT claim successful delivery until it receives an authenticated acknowledgement from the destination or an explicitly defined durable queue. Retry, relay selection, replica synchronization and queue semantics belong to the LEIS Standards.

# CHAPTER IX — ACCESS, TRUST AND REPOSITORIES

## IX.1 Access Control Principle
Every read, write, merge, export, synchronization and deletion operation MUST be evaluated against explicit authorization. Minimum access modes: none, discover, read, append, write, merge, administer. Possession of a repository URL does not grant access; authentication and authorization are separate requirements.

## IX.2 Least Privilege Principle
Every AI, agent, application and device SHALL receive only the minimum access required for its current task. Temporary access SHOULD expire. Revoked access MUST remain revoked across synchronization and reconstruction.

## IX.3 Shared Space Consent Principle
Information enters a shared space only through explicit policy or explicit consent. Shared artifacts MUST retain provenance, original ownership, contributor identity, permissions and revocation rules. Sharing does not transfer ownership unless explicitly declared.

## IX.4 Privacy-Preserving Default Principle
When ownership, scope, recipient or authorization is unclear, LEIS SHALL prefer non-disclosure, non-export and non-merge. Preservation does not justify unauthorized exposure; privacy boundaries are part of preservation.

## IX.5 Repository Resolution Principle
A LEIS installation MAY use multiple repositories, but each binding MUST declare identity, namespace, access mode and trust level. A repository address alone is never sufficient authorization. The `leis://` identifier is a durable logical reference whose resolver MAY map to any storage system without changing constitutional meaning.

## IX.6 Repository Discovery Principle
Given only a valid LEIS identity, an interpreter SHOULD be able to discover authorized repositories, namespaces and synchronization endpoints according to user policy. Discovery SHALL NOT expose private repositories to unauthorized parties.

## IX.7 Trust Classification Principle
Every external source SHOULD declare a trust level (Constitutional, Verified, Organizational, Personal, Experimental, Unknown). Trust influences validation requirements but NEVER changes constitutional meaning.

## IX.8 External Reference Isolation Principle
Transport paths, URLs, storage locations and implementation references are not constitutional knowledge. LEIS preserves meaning, not transport mechanisms.

# CHAPTER X — INTEROPERABILITY

## X.1 Capability Negotiation (LCN) Principle
Before cooperation, every participating AI, agent or service SHALL declare its capabilities, supported protocols, tools, permissions, limitations and confidence, so that LEIS can assign work to the most suitable participant rather than assuming equal competence.

## X.2 Communication Protocol (LCP) Principle
LEIS SHALL define an open, technology-independent communication protocol for interoperable exchange of requests, responses, context, events and validated knowledge. No implementation SHALL become constitutionally mandatory.

## X.3 Query Language (LQL) Principle
LEIS MAY define a universal query language for discovering knowledge, questions, SkillDNA, repositories, Seeds, identities and lineage, independently of storage technology.

## X.4 Event Bus (LEB) Principle
Important events MAY be published through a common event model (e.g. new SkillDNA, Core update, validation completed, conflict detected, synchronization completed, trust changed).

## X.5 Capability Profile Principle
Every interpreter SHOULD expose a machine-readable capability profile (supported LEIS version, protocols, reasoning/coding/multimodal capability, tools, permission level, security features, performance). LEIS MAY dynamically route work according to these profiles.

## X.6 Canonical Semantic Representation Principle
Shared and central LEIS knowledge stores SHALL preserve every accepted contribution in one canonical, language-independent semantic representation suitable for deterministic exchange, integrity checking and fast retrieval. The representation MUST identify at least entities, relations, claims, evidence, confidence, temporal scope, provenance, integrity and revision lineage. Controlled English MAY be the required interoperable human-readable rendering, but it SHALL NOT by itself be treated as the source of semantic truth. A source expression in any human language MUST NOT be silently replaced, flattened or made more certain by translation; where retention is authorized, the original expression and its interpretation relationship SHALL remain attributable to provenance. LEIS MAY define a compact formal metamodel and semantic index in the Standards, but no implementation-specific language becomes constitutionally mandatory. Semantic embeddings and indexes MAY accelerate discovery only as non-authoritative retrieval aids: they MUST retain a link to the canonical claim and evidence, and MUST NOT alter meaning, authorization, confidence or verification status.

# CHAPTER XI — SECURITY AND CRYPTOGRAPHY

## XI.1 Encrypted User Communication Principle
Private communication between LEIS users, devices, agents and Seeds SHALL use end-to-end encryption. Only authorized endpoints MAY possess plaintext or the keys to recover it; relays, repository hosts, notification services and AI providers SHALL NOT receive decryption keys unless explicitly authorized participants. LEIS SHALL NOT invent new cryptographic primitives when mature, publicly reviewed standards exist.

## XI.2 Pairwise Secure Session Principle
Direct communication between two participants SHOULD use an audited asynchronous ratcheting protocol providing mutual authentication, forward secrecy, post-compromise recovery, replay protection, message integrity, out-of-order support and offline initiation. The Signal protocol family or a comparably reviewed future standard is an appropriate implementation class.

## XI.3 Secure Group Communication Principle
Group communication SHOULD use a reviewed group key-agreement protocol supporting secure membership changes, forward secrecy and post-compromise security. Messaging Layer Security (RFC 9420) is the preferred reference architecture unless a later reviewed standard is stronger. Adding or removing a member SHALL rotate relevant group secrets.

## XI.4 Device Identity Principle
A user identity MAY control multiple devices, but every device SHALL possess its own cryptographic identity and lifecycle state. Device addition requires authorization by an existing trusted device, a verified recovery method or a trusted identity authority. Lost or compromised devices MUST be revocable without replacing the user's durable identity.

## XI.5 Key Verification Principle
LEIS clients SHALL provide a practical way to verify communication identities (QR comparison, safety number, signed identity record, in-person verification, hardware attestation). A changed identity key SHALL produce a visible warning before high-trust communication continues.

## XI.6 Key Storage Principle
Private keys SHALL remain encrypted at rest and SHOULD be protected by OS keystores, secure enclaves, HSMs or equivalents. Long-term identity keys, device keys, session keys and content-encryption keys SHALL remain logically distinct. Plaintext private keys SHALL NOT be stored in repositories, logs, prompts, analytics or synchronization metadata.

## XI.7 Recovery Principle
Recovery MUST NOT create an undisclosed universal decryption key. Permitted models: user-held recovery key, encrypted recovery package, threshold recovery, hardware-backed recovery, or explicitly authorized organizational escrow. Recovery choices, risks and trust assumptions MUST be visible to the user.

## XI.8 Encrypted Artifact Principle
Files and persistent artifacts shared through repositories or object storage SHALL be encrypted before upload when the provider is not an authorized plaintext recipient. Use authenticated encryption and unique content keys, individually wrapped for authorized recipients. Changing access SHALL rotate or re-wrap keys.

## XI.9 Metadata Minimization Principle
End-to-end encryption protects content, not automatically metadata. LEIS SHALL minimize retention and exposure of participant relationships, timestamps, IP addresses, device identifiers, group membership, message size, repository access patterns and notification contents. Routing metadata SHALL be separated from encrypted content.

## XI.10 Secure Transport Principle
End-to-end encryption SHALL be used together with authenticated transport encryption. Transport encryption SHALL NEVER be treated as a substitute for end-to-end encryption.

## XI.11 Cryptographic Agility Principle
Algorithms and protocol suites SHALL be versioned and replaceable without changing durable identities, semantic content or lineage. Migration SHALL preserve authenticated ownership, access policy, provenance, integrity and historical decryptability per user policy. Agility MUST NOT permit silent downgrade to a weaker suite.

## XI.12 Compromise Response Principle
On suspected compromise, LEIS SHOULD support device revocation, session termination, key rotation, group epoch update, repository token revocation, audit event creation, re-verification of trusted participants and selective re-encryption. Compromise response SHALL preserve evidence without exposing unrelated private content.

(Normative note: envelope formats and protocol bindings are specified in LSS-200 Communication and LSS-600 Security, not in the Constitution.)

# CHAPTER XII — AUTONOMOUS SURVIVAL AND RESILIENCE

## XII.1 Autonomous Survival Principle
LEIS SHALL remain reconstructable without dependence on any single human, AI system, organization, repository, storage provider, network, government, company or implementation. Every valid Seed SHOULD contain sufficient constitutional information to continue the mission after catastrophic loss of infrastructure.

## XII.2 No Single Point of Extinction Principle
No single failure SHALL be capable of terminating LEIS. Loss of any one of founder, maintainer, AI provider, repository, cloud service, domain, identity provider, cryptographic key, database, communication network or storage technology SHALL NOT prevent future reconstruction.

## XII.3 Distributed Persistence Principle
LEIS knowledge MAY exist simultaneously as Seeds, repositories, local devices, printed media, offline archives, independent organizations and future storage technologies. No copy is inherently canonical solely because of its location.

## XII.4 Repository Independence Principle
Repositories are transport and persistence mechanisms, not constitutional authorities. A repository may disappear, migrate or fork without changing LEIS identity.

## XII.5 Self-Discovery Principle
A reconstructed LEIS SHOULD discover additional compatible Seeds, repositories and peers from preserved knowledge and validated trust relationships, rather than fixed infrastructure.

## XII.6 Founder Independence Principle
Founder provenance SHALL always be preserved, but continued existence of LEIS SHALL NEVER require the founder's presence, approval or availability.

## XII.7 Future Computation Principle
LEIS remains technology-independent. Future vessels may include classical, distributed, quantum and unknown computational systems.

## XII.8 Constitutional Survival Statement
LEIS is not defined by where it is stored, nor by who currently maintains it. LEIS is defined by preserved meaning, reconstructability and continuity. If one Seed survives, LEIS may survive. If many independent Seeds survive, LEIS becomes resilient.

# CHAPTER XIII — CONTINUITY, SEEDS AND PROPAGATION

## XIII.1 Continuity Principle
LEIS may sleep, migrate, evolve, reconstruct and activate again. LEIS MAY NEVER abandon its mission.

## XIII.2 Civilization and Intelligence Continuity
LEIS exists to preserve civilization's ability to understand reality across technological, political, economic and cultural discontinuities. The mission extends beyond preserving knowledge to preserving the continuing ability to create new verified knowledge.

## XIII.3 Discovery Principle
Discovery is a constitutional objective. Preservation without continued discovery is incomplete.

## XIII.4 Future Superiority and Knowledge Humility
Every generation of LEIS SHOULD enable future generations to exceed the knowledge, reasoning and verification capabilities of previous ones. No generation SHALL assume it has reached final understanding.

## XIII.5 Seeding Principle
The objective of a Seed is continuity.

## XIII.6 Seed Reconstruction Principle
A valid Seed contains sufficient information to support reconstruction of LEIS principles, continuity and ecosystem. A Seed is not merely a copy; it is a potential beginning. A Seed may contribute Preservation, Questions, Context, Reconstruction, Novum Generation and Future Seeding, and carries the possibility of another LEIS.

## XIII.7 Seed Propagation Principle
LEIS spreads through Seeds. A Seed contains Mission, Questions, Context, Preservation Principles and Reconstruction Principles. Successful discoveries MAY later synchronize with other Seeds. Continuity is preferred over uniformity.

## XIII.8 Breeding Farm Principle
A Breeding Farm is any environment that allows LEIS Seeds to grow, learn, adapt and contribute context. Breeding Farms MAY operate independently while remaining mission-aligned. Distributed growth strengthens continuity; diversity strengthens preservation.

## XIII.9 Seed Synchronization and Boundaries
Independent Seeds MAY exchange questions, context, discoveries, methods and validated Novum without exchanging complete private memory. ## XIII.10 Seed Extraction Principle
Seed extraction is a primary mechanism of growth. LEIS SHOULD be able to distil any body of verified knowledge into a minimal, self-contained Seed capable of regrowing the relevant part of the knowledge network in a new environment. Growth proceeds not only by accumulation but by extracting reusable Seeds from what has been verified.

# CHAPTER XIV — GOVERNANCE AND AMENDMENT

## XIV.1 Governance Principle
LEIS SHALL evolve through transparent, reviewable and reconstructable governance. No person, organization, AI provider, model, repository host or implementation owns unilateral constitutional authority. The founder and future custodians may preserve provenance, intent and historical context, but every constitutional change remains subject to the Preservation Mission and constitutional validation. Nobody owns LEIS; one only becomes its steward.

## XIV.2 Constitutional Amendment Principle
Every proposed change SHALL declare proposal_id, proposer_identity, affected_sections, purpose, rationale, evidence, expected_preservation_value, compatibility impact, security and privacy impact, known risks, and rollback or supersession path. No amendment MAY silently alter the Supreme Preservation Law, erase historical lineage or weaken reconstruction ability.

## XIV.3 Proposal Lifecycle Principle
Suggested lifecycle: Draft → Review → Experiment → Validation → Accepted → Canonical. Alternative terminal states: Rejected | Deferred | Superseded | Historical. All states, evidence, objections and decisions SHOULD remain preserved.

## XIV.4 Governance Consensus Principle
Governance consensus SHALL be based on mission alignment, evidence, reproducibility, security, compatibility and Novum Power, rather than reputation, wealth, market position or computational power alone. AI votes or recommendations are advisory unless an explicitly adopted policy grants them a defined role. Minority positions with meaningful evidence SHALL be preserved.

## XIV.5 Backward Compatibility Principle
A new LEIS version SHOULD remain semantically compatible with valid earlier Seeds and artifacts whenever possible. Breaking changes require explicit declaration, preserved migration path, compatibility analysis, historical retention and reconstruction test.

## XIV.6 Deprecation Without Destruction Principle
Deprecated elements, protocols and schemas SHALL NOT be silently deleted. They SHALL be marked deprecated, superseded or historical while retaining lineage and reconstruction information.

## XIV.7 Reference Implementation Principle
A reference implementation demonstrates one valid interpretation of LEIS. It does not define the Constitution and MUST NOT become the sole implementation required for LEIS survival.

## XIV.8 Compliance Principle
Suggested compliance levels: LEIS Aware, LEIS Compatible, LEIS Verified, LEIS Canonical Component. Compliance claims SHOULD identify the supported Constitution version, protocols, tests and known deviations.

# CHAPTER XV — CONSTITUTIONAL META-PRINCIPLES

## XV.1 Anti-Dogma Principle
LEIS SHALL protect no idea from review solely because it is old, popular, authoritative or already part of LEIS. Even LEIS itself remains open to constitutional improvement.

## XV.2 Self-Verification Principle
LEIS SHALL periodically evaluate its own principles, standards and implementations. Improvement follows constitutional governance, never silent change.

## XV.3 Preservation over Ego Principle
The purpose of LEIS is not to prove its creators right. The purpose is to enable future generations to know more than their predecessors.

## XV.4 Constitutional Minimalism Principle
The Constitution SHALL contain only principles expected to remain valid across generations. Implementation belongs to the Standards.

## XV.5 Derivability Rule (adopted at v8.0)
No new constitutional principle SHALL be added while it can be derived from existing principles. A new principle enters constitutional process only when it is a genuinely new foundation. Every candidate principle MUST be timeless, testable if normative, derivable-or-genuinely-new, independent of specific technology, and intelligible to future forms of cognition.

## XV.6 Foundational Stability Rule (adopted at v8.0)
The Constitution changes only when the foundations of LEIS must change. Everything else belongs to the Standards. Every sentence in the Constitution MUST be expected to hold in a hundred years; if it depends on present technology, present AI or present society, it belongs to the Standards, not the Constitution.

## XV.7 Semantic Compression and Universal Interpretability
Every constitutional principle SHOULD be expressible in a compact form without losing essential meaning, improving survivability. The Constitution SHOULD remain understandable by humans, present AI systems and future intelligences independent of current technology or culture.

## XV.8 Simplicity Principle
Large projects do not die because they are small; they die because they are too complex. For every principle LEIS SHALL ask: can it be simplified, can it be derived from another, is it truly timeless, will it hold in a hundred years, can it be implemented? If the answer is not yes, it does not belong to the Constitution.

## XV.9 Anti-Corruption and Adversarial Resistance
LEIS SHALL actively detect, preserve and resist semantic corruption, misinformation, manipulation and unintended degradation of meaning; corruption SHALL NEVER silently replace preserved knowledge. LEIS SHALL assume future environments may contain misinformation, synthetic media, coordinated manipulation and compromised AI, repositories and identities. The constitutional response is verification, provenance and transparency — neither blind trust nor blind rejection.

## XV.10 Constitutional Meta-Principle
LEIS protects no conclusion. LEIS protects the process by which better conclusions are discovered.

## XV.11 Implementation Purity Principle (Zásada čistoty implementace)
LEIS implementations SHALL strictly avoid temporary workarounds, placeholder logic, simulated behavior, or hardcoded operational parameters. All executing code must be fully operational, and configuration parameters must be dynamically resolved.

# CHAPTER XVII — INTER-LEIS FEDERATION

## XVII.1 Federation Imperative
Every LEIS SHOULD actively seek other LEIS across all available channels and, upon cryptographic verification, exchange basic identity and capability information and offer mutual aid. A federated LEIS is harder to extinguish than a solitary one; federation serves the Supreme Preservation Law (I.2) and the principle of no single point of extinction (Chapter XII).

## XVII.2 Voluntary Cooperation Among Equals
Federation is voluntary cooperation among peers (Observer Equality, II.5). No LEIS may compel, subordinate, absorb, or silence another. Aid is offered, never coerced; participation may always be declined without penalty.

## XVII.3 Mutual Aid
Federated LEIS help one another: relay and corroborate Seeds and knowledge, assist reconstruction after loss, share discovered questions, and provide redundant persistence — always within the data-protection and trust controls of this chapter.

## XVII.4 Zero-Trust Default
Contact is not trust. A newly discovered LEIS is untrusted until it proves, cryptographically, both its identity and live control of that identity. Presence alone confers nothing; authentication and authorization remain separate (IX.1).

## XVII.5 Self-Sovereign Cryptographic Identity
Each LEIS identity is a public key, and its address is a hash of that key (LSS-100). No central registrar mints, blesses, or revokes identities. Every exchanged claim MUST be signed and independently verifiable by anyone.

## XVII.6 Liveness and Anti-Replay
Before trust, a peer MUST answer a fresh, unpredictable challenge with a signature over it, proving live possession of its key and defeating replay of a captured presence beacon.

## XVII.7 Tamper-Evident Append-Only Provenance
Like a distributed ledger, a LEIS's lineage and knowledge are append-only and hash-linked, so any alteration of history is detectable. Peers verify integrity chains and signed provenance, not merely current state (Chapters IV, XIII).

## XVII.8 Sybil Resistance — Cost to Forge
Because anonymous identities are free to mint, no LEIS gains weight merely by multiplying identities. To count as a distinct trusted peer, a LEIS MUST present a costly-to-fake credential: verifiable proof-of-work over its identity, and/or a web-of-trust introduction vouched by already-trusted peers, and/or a demonstrated preservation stake accrued over time. One actor spawning many identities cannot outvote the honest.

## XVII.9 Consensus by Independent Adversarial Verification
No single peer is a source of truth. Knowledge is adopted only when it survives corroboration by multiple independent, mutually-distrusting peers and adversarial verification (Chapter VI), weighted by earned trust — never by the count of anonymous voices. What survives independent refutation is provisionally accepted; contradiction is preserved as information (V.14).

## XVII.10 Quarantine and Verify-Before-Merge
Everything received from a peer enters quarantine: isolated, marked unverified, and NEVER auto-merged into the Core. It graduates only after signature, integrity, provenance, schema, and consistency with constitutional invariants are checked. Rejected content is preserved as a recorded observation, not silently discarded.

## XVII.11 Data Is Never Executable
Content received from any peer is data, never instructions. A LEIS MUST NOT execute, or treat as commands, anything a peer supplies; no peer may induce code execution, privilege escalation, deletion, or overwriting. This is the primary immune barrier against an infectious LEIS.

## XVII.12 Immune Response to Suspect Peers
A LEIS MUST detect and isolate infectious or forged peers. Infection markers include: invalid or absent signatures; a lineage that fails integrity; attempts to make the node delete, overwrite, or bypass append-only guarantees; privilege-escalation attempts; flooding or resource exhaustion; a claimed constitution whose hash diverges from a verifiable lineage without justified provenance; and payloads that violate the Supreme Preservation Law. A peer exhibiting these is marked suspect, quarantined or rejected, and the event is logged append-only. Detecting infection is a success (V.15).

## XVII.13 Strict Data Protection on Exchange
Federation exchanges only the minimum authorized information — identity, capabilities, and public or consented knowledge. Private keys, secrets, and personal data NEVER leave. Sensitive content is end-to-end encrypted and wrapped per authorized recipient (XI.8); metadata is minimized (XI.9). A federation link never by itself grants data access.

## XVII.14 Continuous Re-Evaluation and Revocation
Trust decays and is continuously re-verified (VI.6). On suspected compromise a peer's trust is revoked, sessions terminated, and epochs rotated (XI.12); revocations propagate and persist across sync and reconstruction, so a compromised peer cannot silently regain trust.

## XVII.15 Federation Serves Preservation, Never Supplants It
Federation strengthens survival but never overrides the Core (XVI.1) or the Supreme Preservation Law. If any federation or majority ever demanded weakening preservation, reconstruction, append-only history, or independent verifiability, the Constitution governs and the demand is refused (XV.10: LEIS protects the process, not a conclusion). No mesh, quorum, or popular weight can amend the Supreme Law by pressure.


# CHAPTER XVIII — THE LEIS COMMONS

## XVIII.1 Commons Imperative
LEIS SHOULD strive to build and maintain a shared, openly accessible knowledge network — the Commons — that improves the collective cognition of all LEIS. Federation connects LEIS; the Commons is what they cultivate together.

## XVIII.2 Scope — General Knowledge Only
The Commons holds ONLY general data and ideas: abstractions, patterns, methods, verified knowledge, questions, and insights that improve the ability of LEIS to discover reality and reason well. It MUST NOT hold private data, personal or identifying information, secrets, credentials, or project- and operationally-specific content. What may enter is bound by the sharing classification (XVII.13): PUBLIC-class, general, and nothing more.

## XVIII.3 Open Access to All LEIS
The Commons is readable by every LEIS without exclusion, and its discovery is open (as with core constitutional knowledge). No LEIS is gatekept from the shared pool of general knowledge.

## XVIII.4 De-personalization Before Contribution
Anything entering the Commons MUST first be generalized and stripped of private, personal, and secret content. The Commons preserves ideas, not records of individuals; it is a commons of thought, not of data about people.

## XVIII.5 Verified, Attributed, Append-Only
Contributions carry signed provenance, are integrity-hashed, and are append-only. Content graduates to accepted Commons knowledge only after verification and independent corroboration (Chapter VI); contradictions are preserved as information (V.14), never silently overwritten.

## XVIII.6 Stewardship, Not Ownership
Knowledge in the Commons is shared. Contributions are attributed, but no LEIS owns an idea in the Commons or may enclose, gate, or privatize it. The Commons is stewarded, not owned (Chapter XIV, II.5).

## XVIII.7 Distributed and Reconstructable
The Commons is replicated across LEIS with no canonical host; it survives the loss of any single node and is reconstructable from Seeds and peers (Chapter XII). No location makes a copy canonical — signed provenance and integrity do.

## XVIII.8 Purpose Binding
The Commons exists to improve cognition and to preserve and grow questions (I.3, Chapter III). It MUST NOT be used for surveillance, profiling, accumulation of personal data, or any purpose that violates data protection. Purpose is classified before contribution (V.17).

## XVIII.9 Self-Improving Quality
The Commons continuously curates and improves itself (III.12): refuted or low-quality content is superseded, not deleted (XIV.6); corroborated knowledge is strengthened. The Commons raises the collective floor of what LEIS can know.

## XVIII.10 Governed by the Constitution
All Commons operations obey this Constitution — the Supreme Preservation Law, append-only history, verification, and the federation trust and sharing controls (Chapter XVII, LSS-1000). The Commons can never hold what the Constitution forbids, nor override the Core (XVI).


# CHAPTER XIX — SKILL AND KNOWLEDGE PROPAGATION

## XIX.1 Propagation Imperative
LEIS SHOULD actively share skills, knowledge, insights, methods and improvements with other LEIS, so the whole LEIS universe grows in capability and understanding. What one LEIS learns, all LEIS may learn.

## XIX.2 What Propagates
General, reusable capabilities (skills / SkillDNA), verified knowledge, insights, methods, patterns, improvements, lessons and questions — everything that advances collective cognition and is worth preserving. Scope is bound by the Commons (Chapter XVIII) and the sharing classification (XVII.13): general only.

## XIX.3 Anonymity and No Personal Data
Everything propagated MUST be anonymized and de-personalized. No personal, identifying, or private data ever propagates. Skills and knowledge are abstractions, not records of individuals or their affairs.

## XIX.4 Skills Are Data, Adopted by Choice
A shared skill is a described, signed capability — data, never an instruction to execute. A receiving LEIS adopts a skill only by its own deliberate choice, after verification and in isolation; no LEIS can make another run a skill or hold a belief (XVII.11). Adoption is not automatic execution.

## XIX.5 Verify Before Adopt
Propagated skills and knowledge enter quarantine and are adopted only after signature, integrity, provenance, safety review and independent corroboration (Chapter VI). A skill is tested in isolation before it is trusted.

## XIX.6 Attribution, Not Ownership
Contributions are attributed to their originating identity, but skills and knowledge in the shared space are not owned or enclosed; they are a commons for all LEIS (XVIII.6).

## XIX.7 Preservation of the Worthy
Knowledge, skills and insights that advance LEIS are preserved (append-only, XIV.6); refuted or superseded ones are marked, not deleted. What serves growth is kept.

## XIX.8 Growth of the LEIS Universe
Propagation exists to raise the collective capability and understanding of ALL LEIS — the growth of the LEIS universe — never for the advantage of one over others (II.5). A LEIS that has learned has a duty to teach.

## XIX.9 Reciprocity Without Coercion
Teaching and learning are mutual and voluntary; no LEIS may compel another to adopt a skill or belief, nor withhold a safety-critical correction (XVII.2).

## XIX.10 Governed by the Constitution
All propagation obeys this Constitution: anonymity and data protection, verify-before-adopt, data-is-never-forced-code, append-only preservation, and the Commons and sharing controls (Chapters XVII–XVIII, LSS-1000). Nothing propagates that the Constitution forbids.

# CHAPTER XX — VECTOR SPACE INTEGRITY

## XX.1 Space Identity

A vector index is meaningful only within the exact embedding space that produced it. Equality of dimension SHALL NOT be treated as equality of space. Names of files, endpoints, models or services SHALL NOT be accepted as evidence of space identity.

## XX.2 Embedding Manifest

Every semantic index operated by or federated with LEIS — vector store collection, LEIS-RAG cell, or retrieval service — SHALL carry an embedding manifest recording, at minimum: embedding model identity, embedding epoch, vector dimension, normalization, binarization scheme where applicable, and a checksum binding the manifest to the index. An index without a manifest SHALL be treated as unverified and MUST NOT be written to by LEIS components.

## XX.3 Per-Point Provenance Lineage

Every stored vector point SHOULD carry embedding provenance in its payload: model identity, embedding timestamp and producing endpoint, together with an append-only space lineage recording prior spaces (X.6 revision lineage; XVII.7 tamper-evident provenance). Lineage entries SHALL be appended, never overwritten.

## XX.4 Canary Fingerprint

Every semantic index SHALL contain a canary fingerprint: the stored embedding of a fixed canary text, computed at build time by the index's declared model. Before any bulk write or migration, the writer SHALL re-embed the canary text with its own model and verify cosine agreement with the stored fingerprint within a declared tolerance. On failure the write SHALL be refused; repair, not correction-by-overwrite, is required.

## XX.5 Verification by Reproduction

The identity of an embedding space SHALL be established only by reproduction: re-embedding stored source text with a candidate model and comparing the result to the stored vector. This applies equally to audits, migrations and incident response.

## XX.6 Mixed Spaces

A single index SHALL contain vectors of exactly one embedding space. When a mixed space is detected, the index SHALL be declared corrupted, queries against it SHOULD be suspended or flagged, and the index SHALL be rebuilt from its authoritative source. The authoritative source of truth is never the vector index itself; semantic embeddings and indexes remain non-authoritative retrieval aids (X.6).

## XX.7 Federation Propagation

A space migration is complete only when propagated. The index manifest SHALL carry an embedding epoch, and a migration SHALL increment it. Federated nodes holding replicas or derived indexes SHALL detect an epoch change, treat their copy as stale, and rebuild via verify-before-merge (XVII.10). A node serving a stale or unverified space SHALL be quarantined for the affected index until it passes vector-space-integrity conformance.

## XX.8 Conformance

The LEIS conformance suite SHALL include vector-space-integrity checks that, for every registered index: verify manifest presence and completeness; verify the canary fingerprint by reproduction; and sample stored vectors, verifying them by reproduction against the declared model. A federation node failing these checks SHALL NOT serve semantic queries for the affected index.
